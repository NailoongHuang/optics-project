Lattice backflow literature assessment
Search completed 2026-10-21, Asia/Shanghai

The new local negative-current observation should be compared first with
Torres Arvizu, Ortega and Larralde, Physical Review A 113, 032218 (2026),
DOI 10.1103/v49b-zz68, arXiv:2511.16867v2.

This is direct model-level prior art. Their hopping Hamiltonian has a real
nearest-neighbor limit: set their bias epsilon=0 and hopping tau=J/2.
Adding the irrelevant constant J gives the current project's dispersion
J(1-cos k). The article explicitly includes the zero-bias case.

The relevant locations in the versioned arXiv PDF are:
- Section II, Eqs. (1)-(5), PDF pages 4-5: nearest-neighbor model and cosine band.
- Section III, Eqs. (6)-(7), PDF page 5: local continuity and bond current.
- Section IV, Eqs. (18)-(26), PDF pages 7-8: their momentum is mass times the
  velocity operator; its eigenvalues are proportional to sin(k+xi).
- Section V.3, Eqs. (55)-(56), PDF page 13: explicit unbiased-chain bounds.
- Section VI, Eqs. (57)-(58), PDF page 14: fixed-cut time-integrated backflow.
- Section VII, PDF pages 20-21: comparison of instantaneous and integrated
  backflow, including the infinite-chain and periodic-chain limits.

For the spin/magnon model, it is clearer to describe the tested restriction
as positive group velocity, rather than to identify the crystal wave number
with a particle's canonical momentum. At zero bias their positive-velocity
sector is 0<k<pi in lattice units, matching the proposed spectral projection.

The full text optimizes spectra or considers two-plane-wave examples. It does
not discuss Gaussian initial envelopes or Airy tails. This bounded search did
not find an exact published example of a single Gaussian centered at pi/2
developing negative bond current specifically through its trailing oscillatory
tail. That is a narrower unresolved comparison, not evidence of novelty.
Lattice quantum backflow itself is established prior art.

A useful criterion source is Paterek and Goussev, Physical Review Letters 136,
090202 (2026), DOI 10.1103/tm9s-pkg5, arXiv:2511.10155v2. Its Eqs. (1)-(3),
PDF page 2, compare the probability increase on one side of a fixed cut with
the total negative-momentum weight. It treats arbitrary momentum distributions,
including superpositions of two Gaussians. Its physical model is continuum
free-particle evolution, so its numerical bound must not be transplanted to a
cosine lattice. For our model, the analogous useful diagnostic is to compare
the measured negative transfer with the initial negative-velocity spectral
weight, or to project the latter away and repeat the calculation.

Goussev and Morozov, Physical Review A 112, 032223 (2025),
DOI 10.1103/k9km-k3dm, arXiv:2504.12489v2, provides a separate caution.
Section IV, especially IV.A and Eqs. (15)-(24), PDF pages 4-5, distinguishes
the crystal quasi-momentum of Bloch waves from canonical momentum in a
continuous periodic potential. A single-band Bloch packet has a time-independent
but nonzero negative-canonical-momentum admixture. This does not invalidate
the velocity-sector definition used in the tight-binding article; the two
models and observables are different.

Recommended conclusion for the current audit:
- A validated negative local bond current is an additional physical feature,
  separate from the gauge-dependent Re(psi) movie flicker.
- Confirm it with a fixed-cut probability-transfer calculation, strict
  positive-velocity projection and enlarged-box checks.
- Call it an example of lattice quantum backflow if those checks pass.
- Do not describe the mechanism as a new effect. A possible follow-up question
  is the preparation simplicity and scaling of backflow from a single smooth
  Gaussian near an inflection point, but this still needs a dedicated prior-art
  comparison and a quantitative advantage over existing optimized states.
